# Pacing a spoken script

## Sentences

- Keep most sentences under 20 words. Put one idea in each.
- Put the subject and the verb near the start. "Ruiz says rents will rise" beats "Rents, because of the rules on new building that the city keeps, will, Ruiz says, rise."
- Drop parentheses, dashes around asides, and footnotes. Make the aside its own sentence, or cut it.
- Do not write "the former" or "the latter". Say the name again.
- Name the speaker before the claim: "Tom Lee disagrees. He says…" A listener needs to know whose view it is before it starts.

## Signposts

- Open by saying what the episode covers and in what order: "Three things from this week's City Desk. Why rents rise, one renter's story, and an argument about interest rates."
- Mark each turn: "First…", "The second point…", "Now the disagreement."
- Close with a short recap of the main points. Repeating once is not padding for a listener.

## Structure the eye uses

- Never read out a table, a bullet list, a heading, or a URL list. Turn each into sentences.
- A list of more than three items is hard to hold. Say how many there are, give the three that matter most, and say where the rest are if the user has a written copy.
- Do not read timestamps or cue ids. The summary file keeps the citations.

## Numbers

- Round for the ear: "about four point two billion dollars" becomes "about four billion dollars" unless the exact figure is the point.
- Give one number at a time, and say what it measures before or right after it.
- Prefer a ratio the listener can picture: "a third", "one in five", "twice as much".
- Write every number as words. See [respelling.md](respelling.md#numbers).

## Length

Spoken English runs at about 150 words a minute. A 1,500-word script is about ten minutes. Ask the user how long they want the episode if they have not said.
