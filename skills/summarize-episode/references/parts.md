# Parts of an episode

Before you write, mark the parts that are actually there. An episode can hold several parts, and one part can appear many times. Leave out a part the cues do not support.

When you mark a part, note which cues hold each piece below. A piece the speaker never gives is absent. Do not invent it. Say in the paragraph that it is absent.

## News commentary

- **Event.** What happened. When and where, if the speaker says.
- **Source.** Where the speaker heard it, if they say.
- **Opinions.** Each opinion about the event, and who holds it.

## Explanation

- **Subject.** What is being explained.
- **Mechanism.** How it works, or why it is that way. Naming the subject is not this piece.
- **Example.** The case the speaker uses to show it, if they give one.

## Advice

- **Action.** What the listener should do.
- **Audience.** Who it is for, or when it applies.
- **How.** The steps or the decision rule, when the speaker gives one.
- **Reason.** Why the speaker says to do it.

## Claim

- **Stance.** What the speaker holds, and who the speaker is.
- **Hedge.** How sure they say they are: "probably", "I think", "in most cases". A hedge is part of the stance.
- **Reasoning.** How they explain it.
- **Evidence.** The data, study, case, or experience they give.
- **Correction.** When the speaker corrects themselves, or another speaker corrects them, or they say where the claim stops holding.

## Discussion

- **Topic.** What the people are discussing.
- **Views.** Each person's view, written as a claim, with its hedge.
- **Response.** How they answer each other: agree, push back, concede, change the subject.

## Story

- **Who.** The people in it, and whose story it is.
- **Setup.** The situation at the start.
- **Turn.** What changed or went wrong.
- **Outcome.** How it ended, or that the speaker does not say.
- **Point.** The lesson the speaker draws, only if they draw one.

## Concrete items

Things worth keeping: numbers, books, papers, tools, products, companies, people, places.

- **Name.** The item, spelled as the transcript or the show notes spell it. If the transcript spelling looks wrong, say so rather than silently fixing it.
- **Kind.** A book, a tool, a person, a figure.
- **What was said.** Why the speaker brought it up, and what they said about it.
- **For a number:** the value, the unit, what it measures, and where it comes from, if the speaker says.

An item paragraph can list several items from one stretch of the episode.

## Check coverage

The paragraph `text` and its `cues` must hold every piece the speaker gave for that part. When a part spans several paragraphs, check those paragraphs together.

When a piece the part normally has is absent, the paragraph says so: "He gives no reason." "She does not say how the story ended." "No source is given for the figure."

A hedge is part of what the speaker holds. Keep it. The Orwell rule that cuts a word that does no work does not remove a hedge. "He thinks rents will fall" and "Rents will fall" are different claims.

Before you write the episode summary, check every marked part. Rewrite a paragraph that misses a piece the speaker gave. The summary must not state a part more completely, or more certainly, than its paragraphs do.
